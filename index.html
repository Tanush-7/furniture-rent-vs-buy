Not everything breaks even at the same time

Appliances hold resale value and rent is steep for them, so they favour buying early. Bulky furniture is cheap to rent and expensive to move, so it favours renting longer. Your selection above updates this table live.

The answer most people on r/bangalore land on

The hybrid play beats both extremes

Don't rent everything. Don't buy everything on day 1. Split by hygiene, resale, and how painful it is to move.

Buy from day 1

Mattress, pillows, work chair

You sleep and sit on these 16 hours a day. A rented mattress is the #1 complaint in every rental thread. A good mattress (₹8k–₹12k) also sells fast or moves easily to your next flat.

  • Wakefit / Sleepwell mattress — buy
  • Ergonomic chair if you WFH — buy
  • Kitchen basics, curtains — buy cheap
Rent first, buy later

Sofa, dining, bed frame, wardrobe

You don't know if the flat, the area, or the job will last past the first 11-month lease. Rent these for 6–12 months. If you renew, buy them then — often the same pieces on sale.

  • Bed frame & wardrobe — rent
  • Sofa & dining set — rent
  • Study desk — rent if unsure on WFH
Depends on stay

Fridge, washing machine, TV, AC

Under 12 months: rent. Over 18 months: buying almost always wins, because rent for appliances is ₹900–₹1,350/month each and resale stays strong. Check if your flat already has AC before adding it.

  • <12 mo: rent all appliances
  • 18+ mo: buy fridge + washing machine first
  • AC: rent if summer-only need

Hidden costs of renting

  • Early closure fee: leaving before minimum tenure can cost 1–3 months' rent
  • Damage charges: stains, scratches and missing remotes are billed at return — photograph everything on day 1
  • Deposit delay: refunds commonly take 15–45 days after pickup
  • No equity: ₹5,000/mo × 24 months = ₹1.2L paid, nothing to show or sell
  • Rate resets: renewal after a discounted tenure is often at a higher rate

Hidden costs of buying

  • Moving: ₹5,500–₹9,000 per full-house move in Bengaluru, plus tip and re-installation
  • Selling hassle: 1–3 weeks of Marketplace chats, no-shows and lowballs when you leave
  • Repairs: washing machine / fridge service ₹600–₹1,800 per visit after warranty
  • Fit risk: your king bed and 3-seater may not fit the next flat's lift or doors
  • Upfront hit: ₹1.2L–₹2.2L on day 1, on top of the flat's 5–10 month security deposit
Bengaluru context that changes the maths: most leases are 11 months, flat deposits are already 5–10 months' rent, and job / area switches are common in the first 2 years. That upfront cash crunch — not the monthly rent — is why renting wins for most first-year stays, even when buying looks cheaper on paper.
Simple rule of thumb

Decide by stay, not by vibes

Under 12 months

Rent almost everything

Except your mattress and pillows. You will not recover buying + selling + moving costs in one lease cycle.

12–24 months

Go hybrid

Buy mattress, chair and appliances if the deal is good. Rent the bulky furniture. Revisit when you renew the lease.

Over 24 months

Buy, preferably used-first

Buying wins by a wide margin. Start with Marketplace full-set deals from people leaving the city, fill gaps new.

Questions that come up in every thread

Is the rental deposit refundable?

Yes, in principle — typically 1–2 months' rent, refunded after pickup and inspection, minus damage charges. Delays of 15–45 days are commonly reported. Keep delivery photos, the agreement, and all payment receipts until the refund lands.

What if I get transferred or move areas mid-tenure?

Most providers offer relocation within Bengaluru for free or a small fee, or will close with an early-closure charge. If there's any chance of moving cities, pick the shortest tenure you can, even at a slightly higher monthly rate — flexibility is worth more than the discount.

Should I buy used furniture instead?

Often yes in Bengaluru. A tenant leaving Whitefield or HSR will sell a 1-year-old full set for 40–60% of new price. That pushes break-even down to 6–9 months. Risks: no warranty, bed bugs (inspect mattresses carefully or buy those new), and you arrange your own transport.

Does renting affect my flat's security deposit?

No, they're separate. Your landlord holds 5–10 months' flat rent as deposit; the furniture company holds its own 1–2 months. Budget for both upfront — total day-1 cash for a ₹30k/mo 1BHK can cross ₹2L before furniture.

What about rent-to-own or buying the rented piece later?

Some providers let you buy out a piece, but the buyout price plus rent already paid usually exceeds buying it new on sale. Treat it as a convenience option, not a savings plan.
